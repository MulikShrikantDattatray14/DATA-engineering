# 8. Employees hired after 2003

**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Track / database:** SQL / Chinook

## Question

HR is auditing the staff hired from **2004 onward**. The `Employee` table stores `HireDate` as text in the format `'YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS'`. Return every employee whose `HireDate` falls **on or after January 1, 2004**.

Output exactly three columns named `FirstName`, `LastName`, and `HireDate`.

Sort by `HireDate` ascending (earliest first).

**Expected output:** `FirstName`, `LastName`, `HireDate` · 2 rows · order matters.

Source: [PrepNPlaced SQL practice](https://www.prepnplaced.com/data-practice), “Employees hired after 2003”.

## My thinking process

I used `>=` because the question asks for employees hired "on or after" the given date.

My initial explanation for comparing text dates was that `>=` treats the text as an integer. This was identified as a misconception during review.

After the question was simplified, I recognized that a hiring year later than 2004 is enough to establish that the hire date is after the cutoff, without checking the month and day.

I then identified that the month is compared next when the years are equal.

## My solution

My final solution:

```sql
select FirstName,LastName,HireDate
from Employee
where HireDate >= '2004-01-01 00:00:00'
order by HireDate asc
```

Pasted Markdown line-break backslashes and trailing spaces were removed; the SQL logic is unchanged.

## What I learned

- "On or after" includes the boundary, which is why I used `>=` rather than `>`.
- Interviewer correction: in this query, the date strings are compared as text from left to right; `>=` does not convert them to integers.
- I correctly answered that the month and day do not need checking once the hiring year is already later than the cutoff year.
- When the years match, the month is compared next.
