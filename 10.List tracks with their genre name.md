# 10. List tracks with their genre name

**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Track / database:** SQL / Chinook  
**Practice status:** Final solution saved; viva closed at 5 questions; join/HAVING relationship marked for revision

## Question

Using the Chinook database, the music catalog stores each track in the `Track` table with a numeric `GenreId`, while the human-readable genre label lives in the `Genre` table. The catalog team wants a quick count of how many tracks fall into each genre.

Write a query that joins `Track` to `Genre` and returns, for every genre that has **at least one track**, the genre name and the number of tracks in it.

Output exactly two columns named `genre_name` and `track_count`.

Sort by `track_count` in **descending** order; break ties by `genre_name` in **ascending** (A-Z) order.

**Expected output:** `genre_name`, `track_count` · 25 rows · order matters.

Source: [PrepNPlaced SQL practice](https://www.prepnplaced.com/data-practice), “List tracks with their genre name”.

## My thinking process

I added `HAVING COUNT(Track.TrackId) >= 1` because the question says "at least one track".

When asked what my join keeps, I selected only matching rows from both tables.

I initially answered that removing my `HAVING` clause would change the result. This was identified as a misconception during review.

In the follow-up, I correctly answered that a genre group formed from the matching tracks cannot have a track count of zero.

In my final viva answer, I said the `HAVING` clause removes groups with a zero count. I described the condition's general behavior but did not connect it to the absence of zero-count groups in this query. This connection remains a revision point.

## My solution

My final solution:

```sql
select Genre.Name as genre_name, count(Track.TrackId) as track_count
from Track
join Genre on Track.GenreId = Genre.GenreId
group by Genre.GenreId
having count(Track.TrackId) >=1
order by track_count desc, genre_name asc
```

## What I learned

- I connected "at least one track" with a count of `>= 1`.
- I correctly identified that my `JOIN` keeps only matching rows from `Track` and `Genre`.
- I correctly answered that each resulting genre group has at least one track.
- Interviewer correction: the condition would reject zero-count groups if they existed, but this inner join produces no such groups. Every resulting group already satisfies `COUNT(Track.TrackId) >= 1`, so the `HAVING` clause does not change the result here.
- Revision point: explain the connection between the join's matching rows and the unnecessary minimum-count filter in my own words. My final query remains preserved as submitted.
