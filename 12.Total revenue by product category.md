# 12. Total revenue by product category

**Difficulty:** Easy  
**Track / database:** SQL / Northwind  
**Practice status:** My final solution confirmed and saved; viva closed at my request after 4 questions

## Question

Using the Northwind database, the merchandising team wants to know which product categories drive the most sales. For each category, compute the total net revenue across all order lines, where a line's revenue is `"Order Details".UnitPrice * Quantity * (1 - Discount)`.

Tables involved: `"Order Details"`, `Products`, `Categories`.

Return exactly two columns:

- `CategoryName`: the category name.
- `TotalRevenue`: the summed line revenue for that category, rounded to **2 decimal places**.

Sort the result by `TotalRevenue` in **descending** order.

**Expected output:** `CategoryName`, `TotalRevenue` · 8 rows · order matters.

Source: [PrepNPlaced SQL practice](https://www.prepnplaced.com/data-practice), “Total revenue by product category”.

## My thinking process

I chose `GROUP BY Categories.CategoryID` because the question says, “For each category.” I used `Products` because it acts as a bridge between `"Order Details"` and `Categories`. I used the revenue formula, including `(1 - Discount)`, because it was explicitly provided in the question.

## My solution

### First attempt

```sql
select Categories .CategoryName , sum ("Order Details".UnitPrice * Quantity * (1 - Discount)) as TotalRevenue
from "Order Details"
join Products on "Order Details". ProductID =Products .. ProductID
join Categories on Products .CategoryID =Categories . CategoryID
group by Categories.CategoryID
order by `TotalRevenue` desc
```

Chat formatting artifacts were removed: escaped multiplication signs and nonbreaking spaces. The double dot in `Products .. ProductID` and the missing rounding are preserved from my attempt.

### My final solution

```sql
select Categories .CategoryName , round( sum ("Order Details".UnitPrice * Quantity * (1 - Discount)),2) as TotalRevenue
from "Order Details"
join Products on "Order Details". ProductID =Products. ProductID
join Categories on Products .CategoryID =Categories . CategoryID
group by Categories.CategoryID
order by TotalRevenue desc
```

I corrected the double dot in the product reference and added rounding around the sum. I then explicitly confirmed the query above as my final solution, with the backticks around `TotalRevenue` removed in my final submission to this discussion. ## What I learned

Review feedback addressed in my revision:

- The first join contained an extra dot in `Products .. ProductID`; I corrected the reference.
- The question requires the summed category revenue to be rounded to two decimal places. I added `round(...,2)` around the sum.

These corrections are visible in my query. In the viva, I identified “For each category” as the wording that led me to use grouping. The interviewer confirmed that this calls for a separate revenue total for each category. I also explained that `Products` acts as a bridge between the other two tables.

For the revenue calculation, I pointed to the formula supplied in the prompt. When asked what `(1 - Discount)` represents, I said I had no idea and had used it because it was mentioned in the question. The interviewer supplied a hint: `1` represents 100% of the original amount, and `Discount` is the fraction taken off. My understanding of the resulting factor has not yet been demonstrated.
